Corporate GHG Inventory

Scope 1 | Scope 2 | Selected Scope 3

Project type: Independent portfolio projectTools: ExcelFramework: GHG ProtocolData: Infosys public disclosures FY2023–FY2025

01 — Objective

Develop a structured corporate greenhouse gas emissions inventory by organising activity data, applying emission factors, classifying emissions by scope, calculating CO₂e and performing data-quality checks.

02 — Business / ESG Question

How can activity data be transformed into a traceable Scope 1, Scope 2 and selected Scope 3 inventory that is suitable for analysis and reporting?

03 — Methodology

Activity DataCollect and structure source data
Data CleaningStandardise units and fields
Emission FactorsDocument source, year and unit
Scope ClassificationAssign sources to Scope 1/2/3

04 — Calculation Method

Emissions = Activity Data × Emission Factor

Results are reported in tonnes of CO₂ equivalent (tCO₂e). Unit conversions are written into the formulas: electricity = kWh × kgCO₂e/kWh ÷ 1,000; fuel = GJ ÷ 1,000 × tCO₂e/TJ; refrigerants = kg × kgCO₂e/kg ÷ 1,000. All inputs come from the Infosys ESG Data Book 2024–25 (pages 32, 33, 35 and 41).

10 — Key Insights

Scope 3 is the largest part

Scope 3 was 79% of total emissions in FY2025 (179,370 of 226,701 tCO₂e). Scope 1 and 2 together were only about 21%, so a Scope 1 and 2 view alone covers a small part of the footprint.

Total emissions fell by 11% in two years

Total emissions went from 254,921 tCO₂e in FY2023 to 226,701 in FY2025. Scope 2 fell by 38% while grid electricity fell by about 40% (97.9 to 58.3 million kWh) and renewable electricity rose.

Scope 2 estimate is above reported

Applying one India factor to all grid electricity gave a result 9.9% to 14.2% above reported. The likely reason is that the total includes US and China offices with lower factors, but the Data Book does not give the split, so this cannot be confirmed.

Refrigerants are as large as fuel in Scope 1

Fuel alone explained only about half of reported Scope 1 in FY2025 (4,295 of 8,745 tCO₂e). Refrigerant leakage added about 4,780 tCO₂e, which brought the estimate within 3.8% of the reported figure.

12 — Limitations & Disclaimer

Limitations: the Data Book does not publish electricity by country, fuel by type, SF₆ or fire-extinguisher CO₂ quantities, or the overseas electricity factor. Scope 1 and Scope 2 are therefore approximations. One India grid factor (0.727) and the diesel factor (74.1) were applied to all years and all fuel, and Scope 3 is shown as reported.

This independent portfolio project is based on publicly disclosed Infosys information for FY2023–FY2025. It is not affiliated with or endorsed by Infosys, and it is not an official GHG inventory, assurance statement or certification. The values reported by Infosys remain the reference values.
